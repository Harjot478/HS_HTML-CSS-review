How I Make Games

The steps I follow to design and build games

1

Brainstorming

First, I think of ideas. I draw out my ideas and think about what the game should do. For the Rainforest Game, I thought about how to make learning about nature fun.

2

Sketching & Planning

I draw out how the game will look and how players will move through it. This helps me make sure the game is easy to understand.

3

Game Design Document

I write down everything about the game. How it plays, what the levels look like, what the story is. This helps me remember all my ideas.

4

Making a Prototype

I make a quick, simple version of the game to test if my ideas actually work. This helps me catch problems early.

5

Getting Feedback

I let people play the game and watch what they do. I ask them what they like and what's confusing. This tells me how to make it better.

6

Making It Better

I use the feedback to fix problems, make the game look better, and make sure it runs smoothly. I keep doing this until I'm happy with it.

Design Principles I Follow

These are the main things I think about when I make games:

Level Design

  • Make levels get harder as you go
  • Make it clear what you're supposed to do
  • Let players make choices
  • Give feedback when they do things
  • Tell stories through the environment

Game Mechanics

  • Make the main action easy to understand
  • Make controls simple and obvious
  • Tell players what's happening
  • Make challenges fair but rewarding
  • Show players how they're doing

Teaching Through Games

  • Make learning happen while playing
  • Make problems that are real
  • Make sure the info is correct
  • Adjust difficulty for different players
  • Test what they learned without being mean about it

About My Journey

What I've learned and what's next for me

My Game Design Journey

I started asking myself, "How can I mix storytelling with coding?" I tried a bunch of different tools like CodeSkulptor and Unity and learned something new from each one.

I started with simple animation and music projects. That taught me programming and how to solve problems creatively. Then I realized it's not just about coding. It's about making games that people actually want to play and enjoy. When I moved to Unity, everything clicked. All the things I learned came together to make real games.

What I'm Good At

Problem Solving

When something doesn't work, I break it down into smaller parts and try different solutions. I use feedback from others to make things better.

Coding

I know C# and can make games work in Unity. I understand how games are built and can make them run fast.

Game Design

Good games aren't just cool to look at. They need to be fun and easy to understand. I think about how players feel when they play.

Working with Others

I know how to use GitHub and work with other people on projects. I listen to what others think and make my ideas clear.

Things I'm Still Learning

I've run into some challenges that made me better:

  • Not Doing Too Much: My first games were too big. Now I start with simple ideas and add stuff later.
  • Making Things Run Fast: Sometimes my games slow down. I learned to write better code and not waste computer power.
  • Getting Real Feedback: I can't just ask myself if my game is fun. I need to watch other people play it and listen to them.
  • Keeping Notes: I need to write down what I did and why so I can remember later and other people can understand my code.

What's Next for Me

I want to:

  • Get Better at Coding: Learn harder C# stuff, learn how shaders work, and make my games run really smoothly.
  • Try Different Types of Games: Right now I focus on educational games, but I want to try story-based games, multiplayer games, and new platforms.
  • Make a Portfolio: I want to finish 3 or 4 really good games that show what I can do.
  • Help Other People: Share what I know through tutorials and maybe help other people learn game design.
  • Get a Real Job: I want to work at a game company to learn from people who've been doing this longer.

Why Game Design Matters to Me

Game design is the mix of art, technology, and understanding people. I love all three of those things. I want to make games that actually matter. Games can teach people, make them happy, or make them think about important stuff. That's what I want to do.

I've come a long way from my first simple animations to making real games in Unity. I've learned that you have to keep trying even when things are hard. I'm excited to make better games and maybe work at a game company someday. I'm ready for whatever comes next.
